# Mobile tire quote checklist

A blank worksheet for comparing tire-service quotes on the same basis. Use it with any provider. It contains no customer records or provider prices.

## Start with the same job

Write down the vehicle, tire quantity, wheel positions, and service type. For a seasonal changeover, state whether the second set is already mounted on wheels. Keep tire replacement, puncture repair, and a seasonal swap as separate jobs.

Keep the door placard specification separate from the tires currently fitted. Record front and rear markings. If they disagree, ask a qualified tire professional to confirm the correct fitment before you order. This worksheet does not determine whether a tire fits or whether a puncture is repairable.

## Use the worksheet

1. Open `quote-comparison.csv` in a spreadsheet.
2. Replace the Provider A, B, and C headings with the providers you contact.
3. Copy each provider's answer into the matching row.
4. Record a fee as `included`, a quoted amount, `not applicable`, or `unknown`.
5. Put `0` only when the provider explicitly confirms a zero charge.
6. Keep the written quote and its date. Do not assume an expired quote remains available.

The worksheet has no automatic total. Adding a blank or unknown fee as zero can make an incomplete quote look cheaper. Use the provider's confirmed all-in total and record anything that can change it.

## Ask this before choosing

> Please quote the same tire model and specification for this vehicle and quantity. Does the total include the visit, fitting, balancing, disposal, valve or TPMS work, and tax? Please list any charge that depends on inspection. What happens if the job cannot be completed, and what are the cancellation terms?

For repair, ask for the inspection or call-out charge if the tire cannot be repaired. For a seasonal swap, confirm whether the price is for wheel assemblies or loose tires. Do not compare those as the same job.

## Privacy

The template is blank. Keep filled copies private. They may contain vehicle details, locations, contact information, or quotes that are not intended for publication. Never open a public issue with a completed customer worksheet.
